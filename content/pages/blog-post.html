PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT · FIELD NOTE 01

A better brief for your next press-on nail collection.

A considered brief gives a product team a useful place to begin. Start with the customer and the idea, then capture the decisions that shape the set.

A collection begins with a point of view: who it is for, where it will be sold and what makes it feel distinct. You do not need every production detail resolved before starting a conversation. A few clear references and priorities can make the early discussion more productive.

Start with the look and the wearer

Describe the intended customer and the occasion for the set. Reference shapes, lengths, colors or finishes you like. If you have a moodboard, existing product or sketch, include it and note which details matter most.

Separate essentials from open questions. For example, a particular silhouette may be fixed while the finish or accent detail is still being explored.

Outline the set and its presentation

Note your preferred set contents, application approach and packaging format. If your brand has visual guidelines or retail requirements, share those early so the product and presentation can be discussed together.

Include the practical context

Estimated quantity, target market and desired timing help frame the conversation. Minimum quantities, sample timing, production timing and delivery details depend on the agreed specification, so these can be confirmed as the project takes shape.

A useful first brief does not need to be long. It should make your direction clear and show which decisions are already made.

A SIMPLE STARTING CHECKLIST

  • Customer and collection direction
  • Shape, length, color and finish references
  • Decoration or signature details
  • Set contents and packaging needs
  • Estimated quantity, destination and timing
